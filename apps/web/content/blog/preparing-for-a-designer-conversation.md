## Start with how you live

Write down what already works in your space and what causes friction. Think about storage, light, daily routines, and who uses each room. A few specific examples are more useful than a long list of styles.

## Gather a small set of references

Save images that show the materials, layouts, or atmosphere you like. Note what appeals to you in each one. This gives a designer room to respond to your goals rather than copy a picture.

## Set your practical boundaries

Bring an approximate budget, a preferred timeline, and any constraints such as a rental agreement or building rules. It is fine if these are not final. Being open about uncertainty helps both sides plan the next steps.

## Leave room for questions

Ask how the designer approaches scope, revisions, and decisions. A first conversation should help you understand their process and whether it suits your project.
